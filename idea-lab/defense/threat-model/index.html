Think Like a Defender

A lemonade-stand website that is wide open. You are the defender. List what could go wrong, then lock the most important things first.

What does a defender do first?

Before fixing anything, a defender makes a short list. Three questions, every time.

📦

1. What am I protecting?

The valuable stuff. Your customers' names, your order list, your money, your password. These are your assets.

⚠️

2. What could go wrong?

The ways someone could steal, break, or peek at those assets. These are the threats.

🛡️

3. What will I do about it?

The fixes that close each threat. These are your defenses. You cannot fix everything at once, so you start with the worst.

✦ That is threat modeling. List your assets, the threats to them, and the defenses. Real security teams do exactly this before they build anything. Now you try it on a real site.

🔎 The defender's first look

Five weaknesses, each tagged on the page. On the next screen you will fix them, worst first.

  • The password is 1234
  • The connection is plain, not scrambled
  • A secret is hidden in the browser
  • There is no backup of the order list
  • The page shares a home address
🔎 Mia's site uses a normal setup: your browser asks, her server answers. Every one of these problems is common on real sites, and every one has a known fix.

You just thought like a defender

You did the real job: you listed the assets, named the threats, and applied a defense for each one, worst first. The site that started wide open is now hard to break.

🧠 The five fixes you made

  • Traded 1234 for a long, unpredictable password that beats guessing.
  • Turned on HTTPS so snoops on the network see only scrambled gibberish.
  • Moved the secret off the browser and onto the server, out of a visitor's reach.
  • Turned on backups, so a crash or a mistake does not erase the order list.
  • Stopped posting a home address, which also makes a trick harder to pull off.
✅ The one habit: list what could go wrong, then fix the highest-impact problems first. You will never fix everything, and a good defender does not try to. They protect what matters most.
