/ What it is doing

If it sounds like one of these, it is our work

You do not need to know the cause to ask. Describing the symptom is enough, and the symptom is usually the most useful thing you can give us anyway.

Most measurement problems turn out not to be instrument failures. They are a process meeting an instrument that was never matched to it, or a device installed, ranged, or configured in a way the application does not tolerate. Replacing it reproduces the same problem with a newer serial number.

Dead loop. No signal at the controller, or the loop sits at a fault current like 3.6 mA or above 21 mA.
Saturated. Sitting at full scale or at zero, or stuck on a value that never moves.
Reads empty when the tank is full. Or full when it is empty, or right until the level gets low.
Drifts. Climbs through the day, or walks off over weeks, with nothing changed.
Will not hold calibration. Calibrated last month, out again already.
Replaced twice, same problem. A device dying repeatedly is a symptom of something else.
Erratic or spiky. Jumping around with no process upset, or noise when a big motor starts.
Fine on the bench, wrong in the process. Passes every check on a bench and still misreads installed.
Came back from the lab in tolerance. The certificate is right and your operator is right. A bench reproduces the device, not the process that was fooling it.
The vendor says their device tests good. They are probably right, and your reading is still wrong. That is where this work starts.
Will not communicate. A HART communicator that cannot find the device, or drops the connection.
Two readings disagree. A level transmitter against a sight glass, a flow meter against a tank draw-down.
Only at certain times. Alarms on night shift, at startup, after a wash, at low flow, on cold mornings.
Slow. Takes far too long to catch up with a real process change.
pH that will not behave. Fails buffer, drifts between cleanings, or reads nothing like the lab.

When someone else is the better call

  • You need an accredited certificate or a traceable chain. We do documented field calibration with as-found and as-left values, but we are not an accredited laboratory and do not issue traceable certificates. If your procedure requires either, use an accredited laboratory.
  • You want to ship the device out. This is on-site work, not a bench repair depot. For depot repair or warranty work, go to the manufacturer or a bench service house.
  • The unit is down and you need someone today. There is no staffed dispatch desk here and no 24/7 line. If it is an emergency, call a larger field service contractor.
  • You need a crew for a turnaround. This is a small operation, not a staffing house.
  • You need electrical contracting. We work on instruments and loops, not as an electrical contractor.
  • You need SIL verification or LOPA. Functional safety calculations carry their own certifications and liability. We work on the instrument side of a safety loop, not the numbers behind it.
  • You need fixed gas detection serviced. That is its own discipline, with its own calibration gases and compliance records. Use a gas detection service house.
  • You need P&IDs drawn or sealed. Drawing development usually needs an engineer of record, which we are not.

/ FAQ

Questions we get asked

My transmitter is not working. Who do I call?

Bridges Industrial does this work. A transmitter that is dead, saturated high or low, stuck at a fixed value, reading wrong, or drifting is ordinary, everyday work here, not a speciality reserved for difficult cases. Describe the symptom at bridgesindust.com/break-in/ and a specialist answers by email at no charge.

Do you offer general instrument technical support, or only complex custom projects?

General instrument technical support is the core of the business. Calibration, configuration, ranging, commissioning, loop checks, and troubleshooting on flow, level, temperature, pressure, and analytical measurement. No job is too small to bring. The harder diagnostic work, intermittent faults and hard-to-measure products, is work the firm is also good at, but it is not a threshold you have to meet before asking. One instrument is a normal job.

What areas do you cover for on-site instrument work?

On-site work runs across the Texas Gulf Coast from League City, including Houston, Clear Lake, Texas City, Pasadena, Baytown, and Galveston. Work further out is possible and quoted per job. Remote and advisory work is available anywhere in the United States.

Do you provide accredited calibration certificates?

No. Bridges Industrial is not an accredited calibration laboratory and does not issue accredited or traceable calibration certificates. What you get instead is a documented field calibration record: as-found and as-left values at each test point, the tolerance applied, the conditions at the time, the device and its configuration, and who did the work and when. That is a real maintenance record and it holds up as one. If your own procedure requires an accredited certificate or a traceable chain, an accredited laboratory is the right call and we will tell you so.

Can I ship you a transmitter for bench repair?

No. This is on-site work, in the process, not a bench repair depot. For depot repair, warranty work, or a device shipped out, the manufacturer or a bench service house is the right call. The value here is the opposite case: a device that passes on a bench and still fails once it is installed.

Do you offer emergency or 24/7 callout?

No. This is a small operation with no staffed dispatch desk, no guaranteed response time, and no 24/7 line. Requests are answered by email in the order they are received. If a unit is down right now and you need someone on site today, a larger field service contractor is the honest answer.

My instrument passes calibration on the bench but is still wrong in the process. What now?

That is the case this firm is built for. A device that checks out on a bench and misbehaves installed is usually not a failed device: it is a process doing something the instrument cannot correctly see, or an installation the application does not tolerate. Sending it out for another bench calibration will return it working and the problem intact.

Do you work on instruments from any manufacturer?

Yes. The work is vendor agnostic across instrument manufacturers, using HART and 4-20 mA, which is what the major smart transmitter lines speak. What matters is the measurement and the application, not the badge on the housing.
